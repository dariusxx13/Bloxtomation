# Hits of 2025

2025 was the year Roblox games broke records that no game on any platform had reached before. Three titles released that year passed 10 million concurrent players. This note studies them and two other defining games. Numbers come from press coverage, Wikipedia (citing press), trackers and Newzoo's summary; they are approximate and dated. For the general pattern, see [Anatomy of a Roblox hit](anatomy-of-a-hit.md).

## Grow a Garden: the idle farm that broke every record

**The loop.** Buy seeds, plant them on your plot among other players' plots, harvest crops that keep growing while you are offline, sell for currency, buy rarer seeds. Seeds go in and out of stock in a shop. Pets come from randomized eggs. Weekly exclusive items require being online to claim. Robux can speed up waiting and buy perks (Wikipedia, citing press).

**The story (dated, per Wikipedia citing press).**

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Early 2025 | First version reportedly built in about three days by a 16-year-old developer |
| April 2025 | A studio (Splitting Point Studios) buys a share when the game has around 1,000 concurrent players; another studio later holds a minority share |
| 2025-05-18 | "Blood Moon" update: about 5M concurrent players, nearly double Blox Fruits' previous Roblox record |
| 2025-05-31 | "Bizzy Bees" update: over 11.7M |
| 2025-06-14 | "Working Bees" update: over 16M, above Fortnite's 15.3M peak from December 2020 |
| 2025-06-21 | Summer update: about 21.3M |
| July 2025 | Two teams dedicated to weekly events; a live event with a professional football player |
| 2025-08-23 | Mock "admin war" with Steal a Brainrot: at least 22.3M |
| By December 2025 | Out of the monthly top 10 by visits (Newzoo) |
| May 2026 | About 35.3B visits; fastest Roblox game to 1B visits (33 days) |
| 2026-06-12 | Official sequel Grow a Garden 2 launches (see [Hits of 2026](hits-2026.md)) |

**Controversies.** The game was criticized for heavy monetization after the studio partnership. An investment analyst alleged bot activity inflated its visibility; Roblox said its analysis showed genuine popularity (Wikipedia). The developer said about 35% of players were under 13.

**Why it worked.**

- A cosy, readable loop with offline growth (a reason to return) and random mutations (surprise).
- Weekly events with exclusive items that require being online, creating a fixed heartbeat.
- Restocking shops and limited items that made timing matter.
- Very low barrier: simple visuals, simple controls, any device.

**Lessons.**

1. A loop can be built in days; the **live operation** is what scales it.
2. Weekly events with "be online to claim" rewards create extraordinary peaks.
3. Records fade. Even the biggest game in Roblox history fell out of the top 10 within months. Plan the sequel, the next system or the next game while at the peak.

## Steal a Brainrot: theft, memes and the all-time record

**The loop.** Buy meme characters ("brainrots") from a conveyor belt that crosses the map; they generate income in your base. Steal rarer ones from other players' bases and run home; defend your base with a temporary shield button and traps. Rebirth for better stats. Admin abuse events grant exclusive characters (Wikipedia, citing press).

**The story (dated, per Wikipedia citing press).**

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 2025-05-16 | Game created (universe creation date per the studio's chart tracker) |
| July 2025 | About 7B visits; a peak of about 5M concurrent players. An officially licensed replica launches in Fortnite Creative and sets a record there |
| August 2025 | Peak of about 20M |
| 2025-08-23 | Mock "admin war" with Grow a Garden; Roblox platform record of about 47.3M concurrent users |
| Early Sept 2025 | One meme character removed after a licensing dispute with its creator and an agency; re-added on 2025-11-29 |
| 2025-09-13 | "Extinct Event": about 24M, passing Grow a Garden's record |
| October 2025 | About 25.4M, the highest reported for any Roblox game; the studio sues an unlicensed copy in Fortnite Creative |
| Q3 2025 | About 26.4B visits in one quarter (Newzoo) |
| By December 2025 | About 30% below its August peak in monthly visits (Newzoo) |
| January 2026 | A live concert by a major pop star inside the game: about 12.8M concurrent players |
| September 2026 | 2026 Innovation Award for a branded collaboration (press lists) |
| 2026-10-24 | About 208K concurrent players and about 74.1B visits in the studio's chart snapshot |

**Criticism.** Reviewers criticized pay-to-win elements (the best items for Robux; server admin powers for sale) and the game attracted automation cheats for collecting and stealing (Wikipedia, citing press).

**Why it worked.**

- A consequence loop: anything you own can be stolen, and you can steal back. Every theft is a story; videos of upset children who lost their brainrots spread widely.
- Meme characters with instant recognition among young players.
- A visible conveyor of rarities; constant temptation and envy.
- Weekly Saturday events that lifted its Saturday averages far above its monthly average (Bloxodes, June 2026).

**Lessons.**

1. **Social risk** turned a tycoon into the biggest game on the platform.
2. Meme characters are someone's IP: licensing disputes happen even for internet memes ([Meme and brainrot games](meme-and-brainrot-games.md)).
3. Theft games need strong anti-cheat for automation; exploiters target the most valuable loops ([Security and anti-exploit](../13-building-it-right/security-and-anti-exploit.md)).
4. A hit becomes a brand: licensed replicas on other platforms, films, concerts and crossovers ([Studio business models](studio-business-models.md)).

## 99 Nights in the Forest: co-op survival with a finish line

**The loop.** A group survives in a dark forest: keep the campfire alive, gather wood and food, craft, explore, and survive whatever hunts at night, with the goal of lasting 99 nights.

**Numbers.** A reported peak of about 14.2M concurrent players (press, 2025; a tracker lists an all-time peak of about 8.8M, so sources disagree); over 10B visits within a few months of launch (Newzoo); about 30.2B visits and about 1.4M concurrent players in the studio's snapshot on 2026-10-24.

**Recognition and brand.** Best Adventure and Best Horror at the 2025 Innovation Awards (press), Best Survival Game at the 2026 awards (press lists); a film adaptation announced with a major studio (press).

**Live operations.** The developer, a New Zealand studio, returned to regular content in mid-2026 with an update roughly every two weeks and a weekly Saturday "Update Party" that rewards players for showing up; it also ran limited biomes with their own currency and shops, and a "biome refresher" that improved weak areas instead of adding new ones (StudioKrew, July and August 2026; Bloxodes, June 2026).

**Why it worked.**

- Co-op with friends against a shared threat; fear is better with company.
- A finish line (99 nights) gives every session a place in a larger run, which most survival games lack.
- Horror moments that make great clips.

**Lessons.**

1. A clear **goal for the whole run** makes survival loops last.
2. Cheap, temporary content (limited biomes with disposable economies) keeps a game fresh between big updates.
3. Improving underused areas can be a better update than adding new ones.

## Dead Rails: distribution as the multiplier

**The loop.** A Wild West zombie survival journey: ride and defend a train across a desert, scavenge fuel and parts, fight the undead, reach the end.

**Numbers.** Reported peaks vary widely between trackers, from tens of thousands to over a million concurrent players, so treat any single figure with caution (RoWatcher, RoTrends and blog sources, accessed 2026-10-24). Created by RCM Games; released at the start of 2025.

**What happened.** Analyses credit a chain: Roblox's own TikTok, Instagram and X accounts featured the game in late February 2025; large Roblox YouTubers posted videos within days; the game climbed into trending and up-and-coming sorts, which fed the recommendation loop (GameAnalytics blog, 2026).

**Lessons.**

1. **Familiar format, fresh setting**: a moving-base survival journey known from older games, reskinned as a zombie Western.
2. **Distribution beats quality alone**: the same game was not growing until it was seen.
3. A game that can be featured needs a clip-friendly hook and a clear one-line premise.

## RIVALS: the competitive evergreen

**The loop.** Short 1v1 to 5v5 duel matches with 40-plus weapons across PC, mobile and consoles; unlock weapons, collect cosmetic skins and wraps.

**Numbers and recognition.** Best Shooter at the 2025 and 2026 Innovation Awards, and Best Studio in 2026 for its developer (press lists); about 18.9B visits and about 272K concurrent players in the studio's snapshot on 2026-10-24, where it also ranked first in the top-earning sort. In the top 10 in 11 of 12 months of 2025 (Newzoo).

**Live operations.** Birthday and seasonal events; many cosmetics come from a concept-art channel where players submit skins that can ship as real items (StudioKrew, July and August 2026).

**Lessons.**

1. Short matches suit both two-minute and two-hour sessions.
2. A strong cosmetic layer lets the meta shift without anyone feeling their purchases were wasted ([Monetization strategy](../09-monetization/monetization-strategy.md)).
3. Community-sourced cosmetics turn quiet weeks into a content pipeline.

## What 2025 taught

| Lesson | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Weekly events produce record peaks | Grow a Garden's weekly updates; Steal a Brainrot's Saturday events |
| Consequence beats comfort | Steal a Brainrot's theft loop |
| Records fade fast | Grow a Garden out of the top 10 by December; Steal a Brainrot about 30% down from August |
| Distribution is a multiplier | Dead Rails after official social posts and creators |
| Hits become brands | Films, licensed replicas, concerts |
| Evergreens hold the floor | Brookhaven and Blox Fruits in the top 10 every month of 2025 (Newzoo) |
