# Icons

The icon is the square image that represents your game across Roblox: in sorts, search, favorites, friends' activity and more. It is often seen tiny, next to dozens of competitors. Its only job is to make the right player tap.

## Specs (platform facts)

- Square image; use a **512×512** template so it stays sharp.
- It is often displayed much smaller (around 150×150 and below), so preview it small.
- All icons pass moderation before they appear.
- One icon per locale (you can localize icons).
- New games get a generated default icon on first publish; replace it.

## What wins the click

1. **One clear subject.** The hero object of the game (the main pet, character, monster, weapon) large, centered or slightly off-center, filling most of the frame.
2. **Emotion.** A face with a strong expression (excited, shocked, scared, determined) draws the eye. Characters looking toward the viewer or toward the action work well.
3. **The promise.** The icon should show what you do or get: a giant pet, a rare aura, a heist, a monster chasing you.
4. **Contrast.** Bright subject against a simpler background; a rim light or outline separates the subject. Saturated colors for casual games; deliberate darkness with a bright accent for horror.
5. **Readable at small size.** No tiny details, no busy backgrounds, no small text.

## Text in icons

- Most casual genre icons work best with **no text**; the image carries the promise.
- Short text can work for some genres (often anime or IP-style games use a logo), but keep it to one or two big words, never small text.
- Text that implies rewards ("FREE ROBUX", "FREE UGC") is misleading, demoted by discovery and may be moderated.

## Consistency with the game

- The icon's hero object and colors should appear in the thumbnails and in the first seconds of the game. A mismatch hurts both trust and discovery.
- Update the icon for major updates and events (a seasonal variant), but keep it recognizable so returning players find it.

## Production

- Render the subject in-game with good lighting (or in Blender) at high resolution, then composite: background, glow, rim light, effects.
- Make 3-5 variations (different expressions, poses, backgrounds).
- Review them at actual sizes next to competitor icons from the same genre (screenshot a chart page and paste yours in).
- Use thumbnail personalization and ads creatives to learn which visual direction converts, then apply the lesson to the icon (icons themselves are one per locale).

## Common mistakes

- Wide landscape scenes shrunk into a square.
- Several small characters instead of one big one.
- Dark, low-contrast images (except deliberate horror with a bright accent).
- Copying the composition and colors of a top game so closely that players cannot tell them apart (and discovery treats it as non-unique).
- Using real brands or characters without rights.

## For agents

- [ ] 512×512 icon with one hero subject filling most of the frame.
- [ ] Reviewed at 150 px and smaller next to genre competitors.
- [ ] No small text; no reward-bait text.
- [ ] Hero object and colors match thumbnails and the first in-game frame.
- [ ] At least three variants produced and compared.
