# Titles and descriptions

Title, description and genre are the text half of your store page. They help players decide, help Roblox search and charts place your game, and help external search engines send traffic. They also affect Creator Rewards: new users who search your exact game name and play can be attributed to you.

## Titles

Good Roblox titles are short, concrete and searchable.

- **Say what you do**: verb plus object often works ("Grow a ...", "Steal a ...", "Catch and ...", "Build a ..."). The player should guess the game from the title.
- **Short**: a few words. Long titles get cut off on phones.
- **Distinct**: a name that is easy to search and does not collide with a dozen similar games. Copying a hit's title pattern closely can mark your game as non-unique.
- **No reward bait**: titles implying Robux or free items are demoted ("Robux! Play now!" is Roblox's own example of what not to do).
- **Update tags** in brackets (for example "[UPDATE]" or an event emoji) are common practice to signal fresh content. Keep them honest and remove them when the update is old.
- **Localize** the title for big language markets if it is descriptive.

## Descriptions

Roblox has published experimental guidance aimed at improving external search engine traffic. A useful structure (adapt it, keep it honest):

1. **Hook (first ~160 characters)**: title, genre, the most unique feature and the main objective. This part shows in search snippets and before "read more" on mobile.
2. **Recent update (optional, short)**: what's new.
3. **Main content**: the core loop and key features, as short bullets or a short paragraph.
4. **Additional information**: supported devices, a code if you use them, Premium or other benefits if relevant, a tiny FAQ.

Rules:

- Roblox notes descriptions have a limited character count (around 1,000 characters for the guidance's purposes), so be concise.
- Do not list controls; teach them in the game.
- Do not stuff hashtags or tags; Roblox says they are not used for ranking and waste characters.
- Keep it appropriate for all ages.
- Accurate: describe what is actually in the game.

## Genre and subgenre

Roblox uses your selected genre and subgenre on your game page and for genre-specific top and trending charts. Platform facts:

- You can change the genre only **once every three months**, so choose carefully at launch.
- Roblox reviews genres and may change inaccurate ones (you can appeal).
- Changes appear immediately on the page but can take days to reflect in charts.

Current genres include Action, Adventure, Education, Entertainment, Obby & Platformer, Party & Casual, Puzzle, RPG, Roleplay & Avatar Sim, Shooter, Shopping, Simulation, Social, Sports & Racing, Strategy, Survival, and Utility & Other, each with subgenres (for example Simulation includes Idle, Incremental Simulator, Physics Sim, Sandbox, Tycoon and Vehicle Sim; Survival includes 1 vs All and Escape). Pick the one that describes the core gameplay; pick a subgenre when one fits.

Genre choice is also strategic: charts are per genre, so a game that is a top contender in a less crowded subgenre gets more chart visibility than a mid-table game in the most crowded one. See [Reading the charts](../15-market-research/reading-the-charts.md).

## Other page elements

- **Social media links**: link your Discord, YouTube, X, etc. through Roblox's supported social links (age rules apply to who sees them).
- **Events**: upcoming events appear on your page with "Notify me". See [Events and seasons](../12-live-ops-and-launch/events-and-seasons.md).
- **Passes and subscriptions** on the store tab.
- **Group** link: the group that owns or represents the game.

## For agents

- [ ] Title says what the player does, is short, distinct and free of reward bait.
- [ ] Description hook in the first ~160 characters; main content; extra info; no hashtags or control lists.
- [ ] Genre and subgenre chosen deliberately (with a chart-visibility rationale); not changed casually (3-month lock).
- [ ] Social links, events and group configured on the page.
