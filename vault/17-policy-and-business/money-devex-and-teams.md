# Money, DevEx and teams

Making a game is half the job; running the business around it is the other half. This note covers ownership, payouts, teams and money management. It is general guidance, not legal, tax or financial advice.

## Who should own the game

- **A group (community) should own any game made by more than one person.** Group ownership makes access, permissions, payouts and continuity cleaner than a personal account.
- Group roles and permissions control who can edit, publish, see analytics, manage ads, manage revenue and so on. Give each person only what they need.
- Assets (animations, audio, models) used by the game should be owned by the group so they play correctly and stay under the group's control.
- Protect the owner account and all admins with 2-step verification.

## How money flows

1. Players spend Robux in the game; the owner (group) receives earned Robux (after holding periods for some sources).
2. The group can pay members: **one-time payouts** or **recurring revenue splits** (percentages of group income).
3. Members cash out their earned Robux through **DevEx** (Developer Exchange) once eligible.

Group payouts count as earned Robux for DevEx if the group's funds came from legitimate earnings. Roblox checks sources.

## DevEx essentials (platform facts, 2026)

- Standard rate 0.0038 USD per earned Robux (since September 2025); a US 18+ rate of 0.0054 applies to qualifying purchases in qualifying games.
- Minimum 30,000 earned Robux; age 13+; one completed cash-out per calendar month; first-time processing takes around 10 business days.
- You complete tax and payment information in the DevEx portal; accurate legal identity is required.
- Older balances earned before a rate change cash out first at the old rate.

See [Platform payouts](../09-monetization/platform-payouts.md).

## Paying collaborators

| Model | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue share | Recurring group split percentages | Core team members invested long-term |
| Fixed payment per task | One-time group payout or external payment | Contractors (modelers, UI artists, composers) |
| Hybrid | Smaller fixed fee plus a small revenue share | Key contributors with shared risk |

Rules of thumb:

- Write agreements down (who owns what, payment terms, what happens if someone leaves).
- Avoid paying large amounts upfront for unfinished work; pay per milestone.
- Revenue splits are hard to change later without conflict; start conservative and use clear milestones.
- Roblox-to-real-money arrangements outside DevEx (for example selling Robux for cash) violate Roblox rules.

## Budgeting

Typical costs for a small studio:

- Contractors (art, UI, music), assets from the Creator Store.
- Ads (Ads Manager budgets).
- Publishing fees or subscriptions (for under-16 audience reach), expedited review fees for timed launches (refundable conditions apply).
- Tools and services (AI subscriptions, external tools).

Plan with conservative revenue assumptions (see the revenue math in [Economy math](../04-progression-and-economy/economy-math.md)), and keep a reserve for live ops after launch.

## Taxes

DevEx income is income. Depending on your country, you may owe income tax and need to register a business. Keep records of earnings and expenses. Consult a local tax professional.

## Protecting the business

- Backups of source code (git) and important place files.
- Documented access: who has which permissions; remove access when people leave.
- Avoid single points of failure (one person who alone can publish or access revenue).
- Watch for compromised accounts (sudden asset changes, unexpected group payouts).

## For agents

- [ ] Recommend group ownership for multi-person projects.
- [ ] Never propose off-platform Robux sales or payment schemes that bypass DevEx.
- [ ] Spending proposals include costs, expected return and are approved by the human.
- [ ] Ownership of assets (animations, audio) matches the game owner.
