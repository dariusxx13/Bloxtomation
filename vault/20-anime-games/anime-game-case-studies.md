# Anime game case studies

Short teardowns of anime games that shaped the category. Numbers come from third-party trackers and community guides unless marked otherwise; they are approximate, sometimes conflicting, and dated so they can be checked. These are studies of **what worked and why**, not templates to copy: the studio builds original games ([Anime IP and legal](anime-ip-and-legal.md)). For the teardown method, see [Competitor teardown](../15-market-research/competitor-teardown.md).

## 1. Blox Fruits: the anchor

- **What it is:** An open-world anime RPG across three seas; collect fruit powers, swords and fighting styles; PvP bounties; a deep trading economy. Launched in 2019 under the name Blox Piece and later renamed.
- **Numbers:** About 60B lifetime visits (RoWatcher, March 2026). Update 30 began on 2026-09-05 with a daily average of about 639K players, then fell on every comparable weekday through September (typical weekly change -21.2%; Bloxodes, September 2026). An Ultimate Balance Patch listing began on 2026-08-07 (Bloxodes, August 2026).
- **What worked:** A huge world with long-term goals; rare powers that create a trading culture; mega-updates that bring back millions of lapsed players.
- **What went wrong:** Long gaps between major updates; a pass catalog (about 5,500 Robux in total, including a roughly 2,700 Robux fruit notifier) seen by part of the community as pay-to-win (RoWatcher, March 2026).
- **Lesson:** An installed base is a moat, but mega-updates spike and fade. Cadence decides the floor between spikes.

## 2. Sailor Piece: the challenger

- **What it is:** An anime RPG in the same pirate-and-powers space, launched in November 2025.
- **Numbers:** About 344K concurrent players against Blox Fruits' 289K, about 186M visits, and about 97.6% likes from roughly 260K votes (RoWatcher, March 2026).
- **What worked:** Weekly updates, usually on Wednesdays; combo combat with cancel windows that players praised; a quality-of-life catalog (storage, rerolls, stat reset, teleport, XP boost, inventory space) and the message "fully playable for free"; active developer communication.
- **Risks:** Weekly content is hard to sustain; a honeymoon rating can fall as the game ages and monetization deepens.
- **Lesson:** In a category with a giant, a challenger wins on **feel, cadence and fairness**, not on size.

## 3. Anime Adventures: the takedown

- **What it is:** An anime tower defense game launched in July 2022 that became a category leader.
- **What happened:** It was taken down after a copyright claim in December 2023 and returned publicly on 2024-12-24 (community reports).
- **What worked:** Polished units, fast updates and a strong summon loop took the genre lead from older games.
- **What went wrong:** Dependence on franchise-recognisable content exposed the whole business to a single claim.
- **Lesson:** A takedown can erase a year of momentum. Original or licensed content is the only protection.

## 4. Anime Vanguards: production value as the entry ticket

- **What it is:** An anime tower defense game launched on 2024-09-08.
- **Numbers:** Roughly 220K concurrent players early in its life (trackers).
- **What worked:** High production quality: models, UI, effects and update trailers raised the bar for the genre.
- **What went wrong:** Remodelled assets after a copyright claim tied to a major franchise in September 2025 (community reports), a costly art redo.
- **Lesson:** Production value now decides entry into anime TD. Spending it on recognisable franchise designs risks paying twice.

## 5. Anime Defenders: the wave

- **What it is:** An anime tower defense game that rose quickly in 2024.
- **Numbers:** A peak of about 202K concurrent players in September 2024 (trackers).
- **What worked:** Arrived when players were hungry for a fresh TD with fast updates.
- **What went wrong:** Newer entrants with higher production value took players in the following months.
- **Lesson:** Anime TD moves in **waves**. Each new entrant draws players from the last; plan for the next wave arriving within months ([Trend lifecycle](../15-market-research/trend-lifecycle.md)).

## 6. The Strongest Battlegrounds: feel over progression

- **What it is:** A battlegrounds game by Yielding Arts with a small roster of characters whose descriptive titles echo a famous series.
- **Numbers:** About 19B visits by 2026 (trackers).
- **What worked:** Combat feel (hitstop, ragdoll, cinematic ultimates), instant play with no grind, and constant clip moments.
- **Risks:** Characters recognisable as a franchise's despite renaming.
- **Lesson:** In battlegrounds, **feel is the product**. A small roster with excellent feel beats a big roster with average feel ([Anime battlegrounds and fighting deep dive](anime-battlegrounds-and-fighting.md)).

## 7. Jujutsu Shenanigans: the late peak

- **What it is:** A battlegrounds game themed on a popular sorcery anime.
- **Numbers:** About 5.9B visits and a peak of about 338K concurrent players two days after a March 2026 update, settling around 145K-186K at peak hours afterward (community guide, 2026; unverified against a tracker).
- **What worked:** A major update with new characters and mechanics created a new all-time peak years into the game's life.
- **Lesson:** Big, well-teased updates can produce records late in a game's life; a game is not "done" after its launch wave.

## 8. Blue Lock: Rivals: anime sports

- **What it is:** A fan-made soccer game based on a sports anime, with character "styles" acting as special moves.
- **Numbers:** An all-time peak of about 276K concurrent players on 2025-09-13 and about 4.55B visits; recent snapshots show about 11K-20K concurrent players (RoWatcher, accessed 2026-10-24). Its title tags change with each update (for example "[CHIGIRI]", "[NEL Aiku]", "[Sendou]").
- **What worked:** A familiar sport with anime spectacle; team play with friends; styles as collectibles.
- **Context:** Kodansha added Blue Lock to Roblox's Licenses catalog at RDC 2025 (official). Rights holders in the catalog can send license offers to games that make prominent use of their IP (official docs).
- **Lesson:** Anime is not only combat; sports with collectible "styles" works. Fan games of IP that becomes licensable face a new kind of business decision ([Anime IP and legal](anime-ip-and-legal.md)).

## 9. My Hero Academia Battlegrounds: the licensed route

- **What it is:** An officially licensed battlegrounds game developed by Gamefam with the franchise's rights holders (Crunchyroll and Toho, per public announcements).
- **What it shows:** The licensed path exists for studios with a track record. It carries approvals, brand rules and revenue share, and removes takedown risk.
- **Lesson:** For an established studio, a license is a strategic option worth comparing with an original IP on cost, control and upside.

## 10. Anime Last Stand: the update gap

- **What it is:** An anime tower defense game.
- **What happened:** In January 2026, community commentary described declining momentum, pointing to premium rerolls and a long update gap. A crossover-themed update in July 2026 was credited with reviving interest (community guides; the two sources disagree on the trajectory).
- **Lesson:** The two most common reasons cited when an anime game declines are **update gaps** and **monetization that feels like a squeeze**. Both are within the studio's control.

## 11. Shindo Life: the comeback

- **What it is:** A village-and-clans anime RPG, originally Shinobi Life 2, later renamed.
- **Numbers:** Every usable same-weekday comparison in July 2026 rose, with a typical weekly gain of about 101%, from a weakest week of about 3.7K to a strongest of about 36.5K average players (Bloxodes, July 2026).
- **Lesson:** Old anime games can return strongly; lapsed audiences are large. A returning-player event, a code and visible new content can wake them ([Retention systems](../10-retention-and-social/retention-systems.md)).

## 12. Anime Dice: a new loop on an anime theme

- **What it is:** An anime game where players roll dice to unlock characters.
- **Numbers:** A daily average rising from about 4.9K players on 2026-09-05 to about 68K on 2026-09-30, with every same-weekday comparison up (typical +43.5%) and official update events on September 12 and September 27 (Bloxodes, September 2026). About 78.7K players in the studio's chart snapshot of 2026-10-24.
- **What worked:** A simple RNG loop (roll, reveal, collect) dressed in an anime fantasy, with frequent updates.
- **Lesson:** The category keeps rewarding **new loops on the familiar fantasy**. RNG, dice, idle and sports twists have lower content floors than RPGs ([RNG and aura playbook](../14-genre-playbooks/rng-and-aura.md)).

## Patterns across the cases

| Pattern | Cases |
|---|---|
| Cadence drives momentum | Sailor Piece, Anime Dice, Anime Last Stand (by its absence) |
| Mega-updates spike and fade | Blox Fruits Update 30, Jujutsu Shenanigans March 2026 |
| Feel beats feature count | The Strongest Battlegrounds, Sailor Piece |
| Production value is the entry ticket | Anime Vanguards, Anime Adventures |
| IP risk is real and expensive | Anime Adventures, Anime Vanguards, renames of Blox Piece and Shinobi Life 2 |
| Fair monetization is positioning | Sailor Piece versus Blox Fruits |
| Comebacks are possible | Shindo Life, Anime Last Stand |
| New loops on the old fantasy | Anime Dice, Blue Lock: Rivals |
