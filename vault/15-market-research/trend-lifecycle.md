# Trend lifecycle

Roblox trends move fast: a new format can go from nothing to millions of concurrent players in weeks, spawn hundreds of imitations, and fade within months. Some trends leave behind a lasting genre (simulators, tycoons, obbies, tower defense, farming, fishing); others vanish. Timing and differentiation decide who benefits.

## The phases

| Phase | Signs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Spark | One breakout game with a new loop or theme; few or no copies | Study it immediately; if the loop is strong and you can ship fast with a twist, this is the best window |
| 2. Rise | Breakout keeps growing; first follow-ups appear in Up-and-Coming | Still a good window for a differentiated take; speed matters |
| 3. Saturation | Many similar games in trending sorts; titles and thumbnails look alike | Only enter with a clearly better or different product; copies get little exposure |
| 4. Shakeout | Most copies fade; the original and a few strong variants remain | Do not start new copies; look for the next spark or a durable sub-genre |
| 5. Genre or gone | The format becomes an evergreen genre or disappears | If it stays, compete on quality and live ops like any genre |

## Being early vs being better

- **Early** wins attention: the first few good takes on a new format get disproportionate exposure while the audience is hungry for more.
- **Better** wins retention: polished, deeper games outlast the early wave.
- Small teams usually win by **early-and-different**: ship a strong core loop with a distinct twist quickly, then iterate weekly.
- Large or slower teams usually win by **better**: enter a format once it is proven, with superior quality, systems and live ops.

## Why copies fail in 2026

- **Discovery demotes non-unique games**: Roblox says games whose metadata and place files closely resemble existing games are not prioritized for recommendations, rank lower in search, and ad campaigns for them may not deliver.
- **Recommended for You favors long-term engagement**: a thin copy can spike but cannot hold play days.
- **Players notice**: copies get dislikes ("copy of X"), hurting like ratios.

## Adding a real twist

A twist changes how the game *plays* or *feels*, not just its skin. Twist dimensions:

| Dimension | Example |
|---|---|
| Theme | Same loop in a fresh, culturally relevant setting |
| Core verb | Replace "click" with "throw", "dig", "ride", "cook" |
| Social structure | Make it co-op, competitive, or team-based where the original is solo |
| Progression | Add a meaningful collection, a rebirth twist, or a base-building layer |
| Session shape | Turn a persistent loop into rounds or vice versa |
| Platform fit | A mobile-first version of a PC-heavy format |
| Audience | An older-audience take (higher quality art, R15, deeper systems) on a kid-focused trend |

Rule of thumb: if a player could describe your game as "X but with a different color", the twist is not enough.

## Urgency read

For every trend-based idea, write an explicit urgency judgment with evidence:

- **We could be early**: few comparable games in the charts, breakout still rising, our build time is short.
- **We would be late**: many comparable games, breakout plateaued or falling, our build time is long.

If late, either change the idea (a different twist or genre) or plan to win on quality with a long-term commitment.

## Signals that a trend has staying power

- Players return over weeks (top-revisited presence, stable concurrency between updates).
- Multiple different games in the format succeed (a genre, not one hit).
- The loop supports long-term systems (collection, progression, social).
- It works across ages and devices.

## For agents

- [ ] Every trend-based pitch states the phase with evidence from fresh chart snapshots.
- [ ] Every pitch names its twist on at least one dimension that changes play.
- [ ] Every pitch includes an explicit urgency read (early vs late) with comparable counts.
- [ ] No pitch proposes reusing another game's title pattern, visuals or place files.
