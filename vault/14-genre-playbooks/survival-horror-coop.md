# Survival and horror co-op playbook

Co-op survival and horror games put a group of players against a threat: a monster, the night, the forest, a killer. They produced some of Roblox's deepest-engagement hits, with longer sessions and a slightly older audience than meme simulators. The fear is shared, so the fun is social, and every round produces stories.

Roblox genre: Survival (subgenres 1 vs All, Escape), or Adventure for story-driven horror.

## Audience

Pre-teens to adults; age rating matters a lot (see below). Sessions of 20-40 minutes. Heavy YouTube and TikTok audience: horror reactions are great content.

## The loop

```
Moment:  explore, gather, hide, flee -> tension -> scare or escape
Round:   prepare (gather resources, build, plan) -> threat escalates -> climax -> survive or die
Long:    meta-progression (classes, perks, cosmetics), new maps or nights, harder modes, lore
```

## Tension pacing

- **Calm, build, peak, release**: horror works in waves. Quiet exploration, rising signs (sounds, lights flickering), the threat appears, chase, safe moment.
- **Sound design carries fear**: footsteps, distant noises, music stings. See [Audio and music](../07-characters-vfx-and-assets/audio-and-music.md).
- **Darkness with tools**: flashlights, lanterns, fires. Light as a resource creates tension.
- **Unpredictability**: the threat's behavior varies so players cannot memorize it.
- **Escalation over time**: nights get harder, the monster faster, resources scarcer.

## Threat design

- A memorable original monster with a clear silhouette and a signature sound.
- Readable rules (it hunts by sound, by light, by line of sight) so players can learn and outsmart it.
- Fair warnings before attacks.
- AI on the server with simple states (patrol, investigate, chase, attack, cooldown); pathfinding where needed.

## Cooperation

- Roles or classes (medic, builder, scout) give each player a job.
- Revive mechanics (teammates can save each other; revives as a limited product too).
- Shared resources and base building in survival variants.
- Communication tools that work without voice (pings, emotes).
- 1-vs-all variants: one player is the monster; balance with player counts.

## Meta-progression

Classes and perks unlocked over time, cosmetic skins, lore collectibles, achievements for surviving longer, harder difficulty modes, seasonal maps.

## Age rating

Fear and violence intensity determine your content maturity label. Mild fear may stay in all-ages tiers; intense horror, blood or realistic violence push the game toward Moderate or higher ratings, which limits younger audiences. Decide the target audience first and design the scare level to fit. See [Age ratings and audience](../17-policy-and-business/age-ratings-and-audience.md).

## Monetization

Revives (limited per round), classes or perks as sidegrades, cosmetic skins for players and monsters, private servers (very popular for friend groups), event passes. Avoid selling survival guarantees.

## Retention

New nights, maps and monsters in updates; events (Halloween is the genre's Super Bowl); achievements; seasonal leaderboards for longest survival.

## Clip moments

Jump scares, last-second escapes, a friend being dragged away, a successful last stand.

## Traps

| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too dark to play on phones | Tune lighting on phone screens; flashlight readability |
| Monster unfair or confusing | Clear rules and telegraphs |
| Empty servers kill fun | Smaller lobbies, matchmaking with friends, solo-viable mode |
| Content consumed fast | Randomized layouts, multiple monsters, escalating modes |
| Rating mismatch | Questionnaire honest; scares tuned to target tier |
